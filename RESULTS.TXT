How to identify a KPI
First questions to be made are:
What am I ahead or behind on?
How far am I ahead or behind?
A KPI requires a and a as well as a  (indicating tolerances like 'red/amber/green')or a (in case of  base measure   target measure   threshold   goal   
cumulative target like total sum of sales at end of year), 
KPIs will always have a target and often they combine multiple measures. For example, you would need to know that 70 people have left voluntarily 
but also that headcount is 1000 in order to simply state 7% voluntary attrition. Similarly, if the main purpose of financial control is to ensure spending is 
in line with budget it's not most important to see where the biggest spend is but instead where the biggest variance.
How to identify which number to show where, i.e. what is target, what is driver etc.
First ask yourself what is the target set on? That metric you will give you 2 first numbers (actual and target)
Then ask yourself is there a threshold or is there only a binary performance measurement (target met/target not met). This will determine 
your Red-Black or Red-Amber-Black colouring. *don't show green for performing on target.
